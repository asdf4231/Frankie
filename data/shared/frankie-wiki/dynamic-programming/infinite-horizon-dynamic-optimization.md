# Infinite-Horizon Dynamic Optimization

> Course sources: [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 5–25, 30–40, 49–52

## Overview

An infinite-horizon dynamic optimization problem chooses an unending feasible path and evaluates it by a discounted sum of period payoffs. In the stationary formulation, the payoff and feasibility correspondence do not depend explicitly on time, allowing the sequence problem to be represented by a time-invariant Bellman equation.

## General and transformed formulations

The control-state formulation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{\{x_t,y_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^t\widetilde U(t,x_t,y_t)\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&y_t\in\widetilde G(t,x_t),\\
&x_{t+1}=\widetilde f(t,x_t,y_t),\\
&x_0\text{ given},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x_t$ is the state vector, $y_t$ is the control vector, and $\beta\in[0,1)$. Eliminating the explicit control gives the state-to-state formulation

$$
\begin{aligned}
V^*(0,x_0)
&=\sup_{\{x_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^t U(t,x_t,x_{t+1})\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&x_{t+1}\in G(t,x_t),\\
&x_0\text{ given}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $x_{t+1}$ plays the role of the date-$t$ control.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 5–7.

## Stationary sequence problem

Stationarity means that $U$ and $G$ do not depend explicitly on time. The resulting sequence problem is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V^*(x_0)
&=\sup_{\{x_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^t U(x_t,x_{t+1})\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&x_{t+1}\in G(x_t),\qquad t=0,1,\ldots,\\
&x_0\text{ given}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For an initial state $x_t$, the feasible-sequence set is

$$
\Phi(x_t)
=
\left\{
\{x_s\}_{s=t}^{\infty}:
 x_{s+1}\in G(x_s),\ s=t,t+1,\ldots
\right\}.
$$

The countable product $X^\infty$ is contained in $\ell^\infty$ only when $X$ is bounded. In particular, the compact-state assumption used later makes $X$ bounded.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 8, 10, 12–13, 22.

## Finite-return assumption

**Assumption 6.1.** The correspondence $G(x)$ is nonempty for every $x\in X$. Moreover, for every $x_0\in X$ and every feasible plan $\mathbf{x}\in\Phi(x_0)$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\sum_{t=0}^{n}\beta^t U(x_t,x_{t+1})
$$

exists and is finite. The lecture notes that finiteness is stronger than what much of the theory requires, but rules out applications in which a feasible agent obtains infinite value.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slide 23.

## Recursive representation and equivalence

The stationary recursive problem is

$$
V(x)
=
\sup_{y\in G(x)}
\{U(x,y)+\beta V(y)\}.
$$

Under Assumption 6.1, Theorem 6.1 states that the sequence value $V^*$ and the recursive value $V$ coincide for every state:

$$
V^*(x)=V(x),
\qquad x\in X.
$$

The result justifies replacing the choice of an infinite sequence by a functional equation. It does not by itself say that the displayed suprema are attained.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 14–16, 24.

## Existence and qualitative results

**Assumption 6.2.** The state space $X$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb R^K$; $G$ is nonempty-valued, compact-valued, and continuous; and $U:X_G\to\mathbb R$ is continuous on

$$
X_G=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in G(x)\}.
$$

The lecture states continuity of the correspondence $G$ as an assumption but does not develop that notion in these slides.

Under Assumptions 6.1 and 6.2, Theorem 6.3 gives a unique bounded continuous solution $V$ to the Bellman equation and an optimal plan from every $x_0\in X$. The later results add the following properties under their stated assumptions:

| Result | Assumptions in Lecture 8 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem 6.4 | 6.1, 6.2, and 6.3 | $V$ is strictly concave; the lecture also states a concave version when strictness is relaxed. |
| Corollary 6.1 | 6.1, 6.2, and 6.3 | The optimal plan is unique and is generated by a continuous policy function. |
| Theorem 6.5 | 6.1, 6.2, and 6.4 | $V$ is strictly increasing in all arguments. |
| Theorem 6.6 | 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, and 6.5, plus the stated interior conditions | $V$ is differentiable and satisfies the dynamic envelope condition. |

Assumption 6.3 imposes the lecture's stated concavity condition on $U$ and convexity condition on $G$; Assumption 6.4 imposes strict monotonicity of $U$ in the state and monotonicity of $G$; Assumption 6.5 makes $U$ continuously differentiable on the interior of $X_G$.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 30–39.

## Two proof routes for existence

Lecture 8 sketches two routes to Theorem 6.3:

1. a high-level sequence-space argument under compact $X$, seeking continuity of the objective and compactness of the feasible set in $X^\infty$;
2. a contraction argument that defines the Bellman operator on $C(X)$, verifies Blackwell's conditions, and identifies the fixed point with the recursive value.

The sequence-space route is explicitly only a high-level sketch; the lecture does not supply the external topology needed to turn those bullets into a full proof.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 49–52.
