# Policy Functions and Correspondences

> Course sources: [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 14, 17–18, 30–35

## Overview

A stationary policy chooses the next state as a function of the current state. A policy function applies when the optimal next state is uniquely selected; a policy correspondence is needed when several choices attain the same value.

## From the Bellman equation to a policy

For the stationary Bellman equation

$$
V(x)=\sup_{y\in G(x)}\{U(x,y)+\beta V(y)\},
$$

a time-invariant policy function is a map

$$
\pi:X\to X
$$

that selects the optimal next state. When the supremum is attained at $y=\pi(x)$,

$$
V(x)=U(x,\pi(x))+\beta V(\pi(x)).
$$

The lecture emphasizes two complications: the supremum may fail to be attained, and more than one next state may attain it.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 14, 17–18.

## Policy correspondence

When the optimizing next state is not unique, the policy is represented by a correspondence

$$
\Pi:X\rightrightarrows X.
$$

In terms of the Bellman maximand, $\Pi(x)$ is the set of feasible choices that attain $V(x)$. If this set has one element for every $x$, the correspondence reduces to a policy function $\pi$.

The value function can therefore be unique even when the optimal plan is not: different feasible sequences may produce the same maximal value.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 17, 31–32.

## Existence of an optimal plan

Under Assumptions 6.1 and 6.2, Theorem 6.3 states that the Bellman equation has a unique bounded continuous solution and that an optimal plan exists for every initial state. Assumption 6.2 requires compact $X$, a nonempty-valued, compact-valued, continuous correspondence $G$, and continuity of $U$ on $X_G$.

The lecture states continuity of $G$ but does not develop the definition or the continuity-preservation proof for the policy problem on these slides.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 30–31.

## Concavity and uniqueness

**Assumption 6.3.** For every $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $(x,y),(x',y')\in X_G$,

$$
U(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)x',\alpha y+(1-\alpha)y')
\geq
\alpha U(x,y)+(1-\alpha)U(x',y'),
$$

with strict inequality when $x\neq x'$. In addition, $G$ is convex in the stated sense that, whenever $y\in G(x)$ and $y'\in G(x')$,

$$
\alpha y+(1-\alpha)y'
\in
G(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)x')
$$

for every $\alpha\in[0,1]$.

Under Assumptions 6.1–6.3, Theorem 6.4 makes the value function strictly concave, and Corollary 6.1 states that every initial state has a unique optimal plan generated by a continuous policy function:

$$
x^*_{t+1}=\pi(x^*_t).
$$

The lecture also states that relaxing its strictness requirement to concavity gives a concave value function, but the uniqueness conclusion is tied to the strict version used in Corollary 6.1.

**Course source:** [Lecture 8](../raw/lectures/lecture-08.md), slides 33–35.
